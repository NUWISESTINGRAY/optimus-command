**Version:** 1.0 working implementation package  
**Prepared for:** Joshua Wallace and the JW Brand team  
**Date:** September 25, 2026  
**Document owner:** Framework Product Owner, to be assigned  
**Approval status:** Proposed operating procedures. The source frameworks remain authoritative until the named owner approves this implementation package.

## Executive direction

The JW Brand Master Framework System is the portfolio architecture. P.U.L.S.E. is the cross-sector operating rhythm and diagnostic. The Enterprise Agentic AI Operating Framework and PROMPTS govern AI maturity and autonomy. The JW Brand Execution Method turns an approved client objective into delivered work. Revenue and Funding governs economic development. Specialized methods and vertical programs plug into these layers. Every live process receives one accountable human owner, versioned evidence, a measurable outcome, an approval boundary, and a recovery path.

This manual is a deployable starting point for a human team and registered AI agents. It gives process steps, handoffs, records, task definitions, decision gates, and implementation sequencing. It does not claim that an agent, integration, contract, or approval already exists. The companion `JW_Framework_Task_Backlog.json` contains import-ready task objects and `JW_Agent_and_Workflow_Specifications.md` defines the control plane and workflows.

### Operating doctrine

Use the source doctrine **Systems, Strategy, Sovereignty** alongside stewardship, wisdom, order, diligence, honest measures, service, multiplication, accountability, dignity and legacy. Treat faith commitments as management conduct, never as an excuse to omit evidence or independent review. Separate cash collected, recognized revenue, contracted value, grants awarded, sponsorship commitments, profit and estimated enterprise value in every report.

### Universal lifecycle

1. **Intake:** record request, entity, purpose, affected people, source, deadline and consent or authority.
2. **Classify:** choose framework, risk tier, data class, owner and applicable sector rules.
3. **Diagnose:** gather evidence and establish a baseline; label uncertainty.
4. **Decide:** propose priority, resources, risks, approvals and success definition.
5. **Build:** execute in bounded stages with versioned artifacts and QA.
6. **Release:** authorized human signs off external, financial, personnel or sensitive action.
7. **Monitor:** compare outcome to baseline, inspect exceptions, close corrective actions.
8. **Renew or retire:** keep, revise, pause or retire the process based on evidence.

The workflow state is `new → triaged → evidence gathering → proposed → approved → in progress → QA → release approved → released → monitoring → closed`. `blocked`, `exception`, `paused`, and `cancelled` are explicit alternate states. An agent may propose a transition; only the named approver may approve a gated transition.

**Separation of duties:** no person or agent should request, approve, pay and reconcile the same transaction. No agent can grant itself permissions, approve its own output, accept material risk, or override the control owner. Escalate actual or suspected safety harm, abuse, privacy breach, fraud, unlawful conduct or material misstatement immediately and pause the affected workflow.

## 4 Standard operating procedure format

Every SOP record includes: ID and version; purpose; trigger; scope and exclusions; accountable human; operator and backup; inputs and data class; systems; numbered steps; approvals; evidence and retention owner; service level; exceptions; fallback; indicators; review date. A task has a single accountable role, contributor roles, due date, dependency, acceptance test and evidence link. Never treat a generated draft as an approved policy.

## 5 SOP 01 Framework registry and change control

**Trigger:** new method, revised acronym, client variant, training request, incident or scheduled quarterly review. **Owner:** Framework Product Owner. **Inputs:** original source, author, development dates, rights record, intended result, stages, scoring, proof, restrictions.

1. Create a registry ID and mark status `concept`, `documented`, `pilot`, `approved`, `licensed`, `suspended` or `retired`.
2. Identify owner, licensor, operator, approved sectors and derivative relationship; flag disputed chain of title for specialist review.
3. Compare the proposed method with existing methods; record why it is distinct and where it plugs into the hierarchy.
4. Write the canonical definition, inputs, sequence, outputs, minimum evidence, controls, prerequisites, trainer competency and prohibited claims.
5. Pilot on representative cases; log failures, changes and measured outcomes. Require independent QA and rights review before use as a licensed method.
6. Approve a semantic version, effective date, migration note and archive of prior version. Publish only the approved copy to the knowledge base.
7. Train affected people and agents; run a regression test on prompts, assessment rubrics and client templates.

**AI allowance:** research duplicates, summarize source differences, draft registry entries and generate test cases internally. **Human gate:** ownership, certification, trademark use, public claims and publication. **Exception:** if exact definitions are missing, keep status `recovery`; use a generic process description only. **Evidence:** source link, redline, test dossier, approvals and training acknowledgment. **KPI:** percent of active methods with approved owner/version; unresolved conflicts; overdue reviews.

## 6 SOP 02 P.U.L.S.E. assessment

**Trigger:** new organization, annual review, material incident, expansion decision or quarterly reassessment. **Owner:** P.U.L.S.E. Steward; **approver:** Executive Sponsor. **Scope:** business, church and nonprofit editions use the same five pillars with sector evidence.

1. Confirm legal entity, location, assessment period, stakeholder groups, permissions, confidentiality and decision purpose.
2. Issue evidence list: mission and strategy; role map; governance documents and minutes; budget and finance; service workflows; technology/security; people feedback; performance, incidents and corrective actions.
3. Interview relevant leaders, workforce and stakeholders. Observe selected work and sample records. Record evidence date, source, reliability and limitations.
4. Score each indicator from 1 to 5 using existence, adoption, consistency, effectiveness and resilience. Use two forms of evidence where possible; interviews alone cannot support a high score. Score no evidence conservatively and label it.
5. Average validated indicators by pillar. Apply five equal 20% weights unless different weights were approved before seeing results. Classify 4.50–5.00 Adaptive, 3.75–4.49 Strong, 3.00–3.74 Functional, 2.00–2.99 Fragile, 1.00–1.99 Critical. Never let an average erase a critical finding.
6. Flag any score of 1 in safeguarding, financial control, legal compliance, cyber, ethics or leadership accountability for immediate executive review.
7. Rank improvements by mission impact, risk, urgency, harm, dependencies and capacity. Put safety, legal, ethics and cash stabilization first.
8. Validate factual findings with owners; the Steward retains evidence-based scoring authority. Issue scorecard, confidence, risks, owners, 30/60/90-day actions and next review.

**AI allowance:** inventory files, extract dates and controls, suggest evidence gaps and draft a score rationale. **Human gate:** final scores, interviews involving sensitive cases, risk acceptance and report release. **Fallback:** paper evidence log and manual scoring sheet. **KPI:** evidence coverage; pillar trend; overdue critical actions; reassessment completion.

## 7 SOP 03 P.U.L.S.E. management cadence

**Trigger:** daily service readiness, weekly operating review, monthly dashboard, quarterly health review and annual strategic renewal. **Owner:** Executive Sponsor with Steward.

1. Collect dashboard fields: measure, definition, owner, baseline/current/target, period, trend, status threshold, evidence date and corrective action.
2. Before the meeting, circulate exceptions and decisions required; do not spend the meeting reading routine status.
3. Review safety, safeguarding, cash, legal, cyber, people and service risks first. Then inspect the five pillars by deteriorating measures and cross-pillar dependencies.
4. For each decision, record decision maker, rationale, conditions, affected parties, communication, owner and due date. Separate unresolved issues from closed decisions.
5. Convert approved action into one task with acceptance criteria. Escalate missing authority or resources rather than silently carrying overdue work.
6. At the next review verify evidence of completion; monthly inspect financial and quality trends; quarterly rescore priority indicators; annually refresh mission, budget, succession and resilience.

**AI allowance:** compile dashboard, flag threshold breaches, propose agenda and draft minutes. **Human gate:** decisions, material external messages and risk acceptance. **KPI:** action closure on time, decision latency, recurring exceptions, five-pillar trends.

## 8 SOP 04 JW Brand Execution Method

**Trigger:** signed scope and completed intake. **Owner:** Delivery Lead. **Five gates:** Clarify Vision → Build the Brand → Engineer the System → Launch the Market → Scale the Operation.

1. **Clarify Vision:** validate purpose, audience, current condition, constraints, measures, authority and budget. Use CLEAR and 5Cs only where the relevant approved module applies. Gate: client signs off the problem, baseline, outcomes and scope.
2. **Build the Brand:** create position, promise, offer, message, identity and authority assets. Use BRAND and CREATE Brand Development as defined in the registry. Document rights and accessibility. Gate: brand owner accepts controlled assets and claims.
3. **Engineer the System:** map roles, data, client journey, SOPs, technology, finance, service levels, security and fallback. Gate: process owners test an end-to-end normal and exception case.
4. **Launch the Market:** approve campaign, channel, pipeline, pricing, handoff and fulfillment capacity. Use CREATE Marketing where approved. Gate: authorized client representative approves public release and the team demonstrates readiness.
5. **Scale the Operation:** compare baseline to results, fix rework, train replacements, automate low-risk tasks, establish product support and licensing conditions. Gate: documented performance and control evidence supports expanded capacity.

At each gate capture owner, deliverable version, acceptance criteria, client decision and rework. A stage can be repeated. **AI allowance:** draft, research, produce variants, test consistency, map process. **Human gate:** strategic decisions, client acceptance, public launch, spend and contract changes. **KPI:** time to stage acceptance, rework, client outcomes, margin and adoption.

## 9 SOP 05 Brand and marketing modules

**CREATE Brand Development:** Clarify, Refine, Establish, Activate, Transform, Expand. **CREATE Marketing:** Content, Relationships, Education, Awareness, Traffic, Engagement. These are distinct module versions; store different IDs and do not let the same acronym overwrite the other. **CLEAR:** Clarify, Leverage, Execute, Align, Reinforce. **BRAND:** Blueprint, Refine, Align, Name, Differentiate. **5Cs:** Clarity, Character, Consistency, Connection, Credibility.

1. Select one module and document the problem it solves and the approved version.
2. Capture audience evidence, current brand assets, rights, baseline response and required channels.
3. Produce the module's stage outputs: decision brief, audience/offer/message architecture, asset plan, activation calendar or scorecard as applicable.
4. Run fact, spelling, rights, brand, accessibility, channel and production preflight. Verify claims against an approved source.
5. Seek brand/client approval for release; preserve final files and source provenance.
6. Observe qualified reach, engagement, conversion, experience feedback and cost. Record changes to the message or channel with rationale.

**AI allowance:** research, draft concepts and variants, preflight and reporting. **Human gate:** identity adoption, offer/pricing, campaign release, sensitive claims and talent likeness. **Fallback:** use prior approved assets and manual channel checklist. **KPI:** claim correction rate, brand consistency, qualified pipeline and conversion. For Communication DNA, Kingdom Brand Architecture, PASSION and 5P, follow SOP 01 until exact canonical definitions are recovered.

## 10 SOP 06 Revenue and Funding System

**Trigger:** new lead, sponsor prospect, grant opportunity, renewal, cash variance or strategic funding review. **Owner:** Revenue Lead; **finance control:** Finance Lead.

1. Define the offer and economic unit: price, direct cost, capacity, payment terms, margin and delivery owner. Approve claims and allowable sources.
2. Qualify opportunity using fit, decision authority, budget or funding rules, timeline, restrictions, probability and next action. Record the source of each fact.
3. Produce a scoped proposal or grant/sponsor package with deliverables, exclusions, rights, payment milestones, performance measures and approval route.
4. Have a human approve pricing, commitments, grant eligibility and external submission. Record signed documents in the system of record.
5. Handoff to Delivery with scope, obligations, activation calendar, reporting rules and invoice schedule; confirm receipt.
6. Finance reconciles invoicing, cleared cash, recognized revenue, restricted funds, sponsor fulfillment and receivables separately. No AI-generated assertion of a grant award or sponsorship sale without an executed source.
7. Review pipeline aging weekly, cash and margin monthly, obligations and renewal windows at defined intervals. Correct slippage and close or archive lost opportunities with reason.

**AI allowance:** prospect research from approved sources, eligibility screening, draft proposals, reminders and variance flags. **Human gate:** outreach/send, financial numbers, legal terms, submission, commitments, payments and grants. **Fallback:** controlled spreadsheet plus contract and bank reconciliation. **KPI:** qualified pipeline, conversion, collections, margin, sponsor fulfillment and restricted-fund compliance.

## 11 SOP 07 PROMPTS maturity and AI use-case intake

**Trigger:** proposed AI tool, agent, automation, model change or new data use. **Owner:** AI Governance Lead with process owner.

1. Register purpose, users, baseline, expected benefit, affected people, data classes, permissions, cost, vendor and alternatives.
2. Assign risk tier: Tier 1 internal reversible work; Tier 2 client drafts and moderate recommendations; Tier 3 material pricing, publishing, purchases, personnel support, records changes or production release; Tier 4 restricted legal determinations, biometric identification, autonomous payment, unrestricted deletion, covert persuasion or safety-critical control. Tier 4 requires a specifically authorized lawful exception and independent review or remains prohibited.
3. Select current PROMPTS level. **Pick:** approved tools and data controls. **Refine:** versioned instructions and representative evaluations. **Operate:** tested end-to-end integrations. **Make:** rights, accessibility and production preflight. **Productize:** owner, support, economics and security. **Trigger:** reliable events, duplicate protection, pause/replay/cancel. **Synthesize:** registered agents and bounded orchestration.
4. Document advancement evidence and test normal, duplicate, delayed, failed, malicious and recovery cases as applicable. Do not advance solely because a demonstration worked.
5. Obtain tier-based business, security, data, legal and AI approvals. Pilot with bounded users, volume and duration. Compare to baseline.
6. Monitor quality, cost, drift, incidents and adoption; review material changes before release; retire identities, triggers and data correctly.

**Human gate:** all Tier 3 actions; Tier 4 exception; any external action not specifically authorized in a tested policy. **KPI:** registered coverage, severe error, override, cost per successful task, incident rate and recovery time.

## 12 SOP 08 Agent build, operation and retirement

**Owner:** AI Product Owner and named business process owner. Each agent card must specify mission, unique identity/version, approved context, model and prompt, tools/parameters, memory, permissions, data class, limits, stop conditions, approvals, evaluation set, telemetry, fallback and kill switch.

1. Intake and classify use case; prohibit unapproved data and external access.
2. Design bounded task and structured input/output schema with provenance and uncertainty fields.
3. Build in an isolated test environment using least privilege, secret storage and separate service identities.
4. Test factuality, instructions from untrusted content, privacy, cross-client leakage, bias, rights, prompt injection, tool failure, retries, cost, latency and shutdown.
5. Pilot under volume/time caps with human sampling; log all outputs, tool calls, approvals, failures and revisions.
6. Release only against signed gate conditions. Monitor thresholds; pause on severe error, unauthorized action, safety or privacy event, or control failure.
7. Change model, prompt, data or tools through impact assessment and regression. Retire by disabling credentials/triggers, exporting required records and recording deletion/retention disposition.

**Exception handling:** if output lacks evidence, return `insufficient_evidence` and create a human review task. If an action fails, retry only when idempotent and within the approved cap; otherwise route to an exception queue. **KPI:** groundedness, error severity, action traceability, human override, cycle time, unit cost and recovery.

## 13 SOP 09 Data, security and assurance

**Owner:** Security/Data Lead; control owners retain subject-specific accountability. The enterprise source names sixteen assurance disciplines: data architecture, cybersecurity, client confidentiality, intellectual property protection, AI governance, human approval, accuracy testing, regulatory compliance, integration architecture, financial return, employee adoption, change management, backup procedures, vendor dependency, model monitoring and performance metrics. Accessibility is a requirement within regulatory and service design, not a separately numbered discipline in that source.

1. Map authoritative systems, data flows, classifications, collection basis, retention, deletion and client boundaries.
2. Grant least-privilege access with MFA, logging, named owner and periodic review. Keep credentials out of prompts.
3. Evaluate confidentiality, rights, accuracy, accessibility and applicable obligations before deployment.
4. Validate integration schemas, authentication, duplicate behavior, audit IDs and manual fallback.
5. Maintain encrypted backups and test restoration of data, prompts, configuration and essential records.
6. Define alerts for performance, model drift, cost and incident thresholds. Pause affected processing and notify process/control owners when confidentiality, safety, legal rights, payment, production integrity or material commitments may be harmed.
7. Review vendor concentration, export and exit capability; log exceptions with owner, expiry and compensating control.

**AI allowance:** inventory, detect anomalies and assemble evidence. **Human gate:** access grant, risk acceptance, breach response, legal interpretation and release. **KPI:** current access reviews, restore success, unresolved critical findings and vendor exit readiness.

## 21 Recovery SOP for incomplete methods

## 23 Ninety-day implementation

**Days 1–14, govern and baseline:** assign the six core owners; confirm contracting/licensing entities; freeze architecture; recover incomplete definitions; inventory current projects and AI tools; approve risk matrix; select three to five paid pilots; build the three diagnostic forms and proof structure; establish CRM/board and weekly review. Exit: accountable roles, register, controlled intake, pilot scopes and baselines.

**Days 15–30, document and test:** complete five-stage delivery cards; configure task fields and dashboards; write critical SOPs for revenue, service, finance, data and incident response; develop bounded agent prototypes for intake, research and QA; run representative tests and fallback drill; begin paid pilot work. Exit: passing normal/exception tests and documented approvals.

**Days 31–60, operate and correct:** deliver stage gates, monitor quality/cost, capture client decisions, correct agent and human process failures, train backups, produce first verified proof points, review funding and capacity. Exit: repeatable delivery evidence and material exceptions addressed.

**Days 61–90, scale selectively:** reassess pilots, approve updated versions, run certification beta only for fully defined methods, test license and support terms, expand automation only after controls pass, choose next-quarter portfolio based on cash, margin, outcomes and risk. Exit: approved next-quarter backlog, refreshed scorecard and management review.

### Launch gates

**Gate A, identity:** actual legal entity, rights holder, roles and permissions confirmed. **Gate B, methods:** canonical source and acceptance rubric approved. **Gate C, systems:** data map, access, backup, audit and fallback tested. **Gate D, agents:** registry, risk tier, evaluations, budget and kill authority. **Gate E, commercial:** signed terms, pricing, capacity and finance reconciliation. **Gate F, sector:** safeguarding, eligibility, rules, insurance or other program obligations reviewed.
